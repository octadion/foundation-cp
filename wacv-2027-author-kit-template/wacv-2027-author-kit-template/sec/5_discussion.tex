\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

A per-class conformal correction can be predicted from calibration-label-free descriptors and
applied to classes with no calibration examples at all. On ImageNet it takes $70\%$ of an
oracle ceiling there, improves on every existing method we could run, two of which are
undefined for all such classes, and needs labels for as few as a tenth of the classes.
Marginal coverage is preserved because a single scalar is calibrated
(\cref{prop:marginal}).

\paragraph{Limitations.} The claim at $n_y=0$ is empirical, for the reason
\cref{sec:correction} gives. The improvement attenuates for adaptive scores and disappears at
$\alpha=0.01$. Transfer across architectures remains
untested, since the additional backbones leave $35$ evaluation examples per class, which
\cref{fig:sweeps}a places below the depth at which anything is detectable.
The per-split spread behind every percentage of the ceiling is wide, and five splits is
what ImageNet's validation set supports at this depth. The depth at which the ceiling
collapses was measured on one dataset at one $\alpha$, and we would not expect a single number
to transfer exactly. Finally, every table uses a
random per-class subsample of the score dump, adopted for memory reasons
(\cref{app:rowbudget}); sweeping it over a factor of three moves the improvement by less than
the interval of any single setting, so the headline is not an artefact of it, but we cannot
say what happens on the full dump.

\paragraph{Evaluating class-conditional coverage.} The observation we expect to outlast this
method is that worst-class coverage becomes unmeasurable before it becomes unachievable. Below
roughly $35$ evaluation examples per class the oracle ceiling is zero, so a null result on such
a slice says little about the method that produced it, and \cref{fig:sweeps}a shows how much of
each long-tailed benchmark sits there. Published comparisons on them may report a property of
the evaluation set where they intend a difference between methods. We state the measurability
regime beside every number, and three of our own explanations were discarded before we
understood this (\cref{sec:depth}).

\paragraph{Future work.} The most direct next step is a larger evaluation set for the rare
classes themselves, since the restriction in \cref{tab:longtail} keeps under a tenth of the
label space. A second question is why $\varphi$ carries signal at $K=1081$ classes and not at
$K=8142$; the failure is in the regression's accuracy rather than its form
(\cref{app:longtail,app:heads}). The
most interesting direction is a formal statement to replace the empirical one. No procedure can
bound coverage for an individual class with $n_y=0$, but group-conditional conformal prediction
can bound it for a neighbourhood of classes, and the framework of
\citet{bairaktari2025kandinsky} accepts overlapping, fractional groups defined jointly on
covariates and labels, which is the shape our descriptors have. Groups drawn in
$\varphi$-space would give a guarantee about regions of the classifier's output geometry:
weaker than a per-class statement, and much stronger than none.
