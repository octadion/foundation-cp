\section{Predicted Class Correction}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

Let $(X_i,Y_i)_{i=1}^{n}$ be calibration pairs and $(X_{n+1},Y_{n+1})$ a test pair, all
exchangeable, with labels in $\mathcal{Y}=\{1,\dots,K\}$. A conformal score
$s:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{R}$ is larger when a pair conforms less; we use
$s(x,y)=1-\hat p(y\mid x)$, the LAC score of \citet{sadinle2019least}, and report APS
\cite{romano2020classification}, RAPS \cite{angelopoulos2021uncertainty} and SAPS
\cite{huang2024conformal} in \cref{tab:depth}. Write $n_y$ for the number of calibration
examples of class $y$, and let
\begin{equation}
\hat q = \mathrm{Quantile}\Big(\{s(X_i,Y_i)\}_{i=1}^n;\ \tfrac{\lceil (n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil}{n}\Big)
\label{eq:qglobal}
\end{equation}
be the marginal conformal quantile. Classwise conformal prediction replaces $\hat q$ by a
per-class quantile $\hat q_y$ computed from the $n_y$ examples of class $y$; equivalently it
applies a per-class \emph{offset}
\begin{equation}
\delta_y \;=\; \hat q_y - \hat q ,
\label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}
which is positive for classes that need a looser threshold. When $n_y=0$, $\hat q_y$ and
hence $\delta_y$ are undefined.

\paragraph{Held-out classes.} We study the zero-example case directly. A fraction of classes
is designated as \emph{held out} and all of their calibration rows are deleted, so that
$n_y$ is exactly zero for them. Results are then reported in two tables that
are never averaged together, one for the classes that kept their data and one for the held-out
classes, since a method that wins on the held-out classes by sacrificing the others has not
solved the problem and a single pooled number would conceal that.

\paragraph{Choice of intervention.} A natural alternative is to improve
$s(x,y)$ itself, whether by a better model, a better calibration of its probabilities, or by
aggregating predictions over transformed copies of the input. Any such intervention reshapes
the score for all classes at once. Class-conditional coverage, however, is set by where each
class's threshold falls in its own score distribution, and a uniformly better score moves
every class in the same direction. Reducing the spread of per-class coverage therefore requires a
degree of freedom per class, which $\delta_y$ supplies and a score-level intervention does
not. \Cref{app:score-level} makes this concrete with a measurement.

\paragraph{Matched set size.} Coverage can always be bought with larger sets, so comparing
coverage at unmatched set size compares nothing. Every method we report, ours included, is
shifted by one global constant until its \emph{average set size} equals a common target, and
worst-class coverage is read at that matched size. This is the resource-matched comparison
used throughout \cref{sec:exp}.

\subsection{Calibration-label-free class descriptors}
\label{sec:phi}

For a linear classifier head with weight rows $w_y\in\mathbb{R}^d$ and biases $b_y$, we form a
descriptor vector $\varphi(y)\in\mathbb{R}^{p}$, $p=9$, from
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt,leftmargin=*]
\item $\|w_y\|_2$ and $b_y$, the scale and offset of the class logit;
\item cosine distances from $w_y$ to its $k$ nearest competing rows for
      $k\in\{5,10,50\}$, the distance to the nearest row, the spread of that
      neighbourhood, and the cosine distance to the mean row, all of which measure how
      crowded the class is;
\item $\log$ of the class prevalence in the training set.
\end{enumerate}
The term we use is \emph{calibration-label-free}, and what separates it from label-free is
the prevalence feature. The eight geometric features are functions of the model's parameters
alone, so they are defined for a class with no examples of any kind and carry no sampling
noise. Prevalence is counted from the \emph{training} labels, which are available whenever the
model is, but which are labels; it is therefore free of \emph{calibration} labels and not free
of labels altogether. Nothing in the method depends on it: removing prevalence costs $0.011$
in worst-class coverage and does not reach significance ($p=0.30$), while prevalence on its own
is significantly worse than the full set (\cref{tab:ablation}). The feature that most closely
resembles the regression target is held out of the feature set, so the reported numbers cannot
be attributed to that one feature.

\subsection{The correction}
\label{sec:correction}

\paragraph{Step 1: regress.} On the classes with at least $n_{\mathrm{cal}}$ calibration
examples, a count fixed in advance, we observe $\delta_y$ from
\cref{eq:delta} and fit a ridge regression $g_\theta:\mathbb{R}^p\to\mathbb{R}$ to predict it
from $\varphi(y)$. There is no network and no training loop: $g_\theta$ is a closed-form fit
over at most $K$ points with $p\le 15$ features, which costs milliseconds
(\cref{tab:runtime}).

\paragraph{Step 2: shrink.} The raw prediction is applied with a scalar $\lambda$,
\begin{equation}
\tilde\delta_y \;=\; \lambda\cdot g_\theta(\varphi(y)) ,
\label{eq:shrink}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda$ selected on the labelled label space only, by maximising worst-class coverage
at matched size over a fixed grid. This restriction matters, because choosing $\lambda$ with
help from the held-out classes would allow a free parameter to manufacture the very result the
held-out table is meant to test, and it would also void \cref{prop:marginal}.

The shrinkage step is what makes the method usable. Worst-class coverage is governed by the
largest error in $\tilde\delta$, which its mean squared error does not control, so a predictor
with a respectable $R^2$ can still be harmful when applied at full strength. Measured on ImageNet,
$\lambda=1$ costs $0.584$ in worst-class coverage relative to the selected
$\lambda\approx0.1$, while $\lambda=0$ recovers the marginal threshold exactly
(\cref{tab:ablation}). Both endpoints of the grid are significantly worse than its interior.

\paragraph{Accuracy required of $g_\theta$.} We have no theory saying when $\varphi$
predicts $\delta$ well, and the honest summary is that the method works exactly as far as the
regression does. Our three datasets happen to span a wide range of regression quality and line
up monotonically with the outcome: the cross-validated mean squared error of $g_\theta$ is
$0.036$ on ImageNet, $0.072$ on Pl@ntNet-300K and $0.127$ on iNaturalist-2018, against
worst-class improvements of $0.058$, $0.019$ and $0.000$. The shrinkage step is what makes a
poor regression safe instead of harmful, since it can select $\lambda=0$ and return the
marginal threshold, and on iNaturalist-2018 that is what happens in every split
(\cref{sec:depth}). A practitioner can therefore read $\lambda$ as a diagnostic: it reports how
much of $g_\theta$ the calibration data was willing to trust.

\paragraph{Step 3: recalibrate.} Finally one scalar $c$ is refit, on labelled-class rows only,
so that marginal coverage returns to $1-\alpha$. The prediction set is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}(x) \;=\; \bigl\{\, y : s(x,y) \le \hat q + \tilde\delta_y + c \,\bigr\}.
\label{eq:set}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Data splitting}
\label{sec:splits}

Because every quantity above is estimated, it matters exactly which rows and which classes each
one is allowed to see. Two independent partitions are used. Classes are partitioned into
\emph{labelled} classes $\mathcal{Y}_{\mathrm{lab}}$ and \emph{held-out} classes
$\mathcal{Y}_{\mathrm{ho}}$, and, independently, the rows of every class are partitioned into a
calibration part and an evaluation part. \Cref{tab:splits} states which combination each
estimated quantity is fitted on.

Two invariants hold in all our experiments. Nothing is ever fitted on evaluation rows, and
nothing is ever fitted on a held-out class. Both are enforced in code by reindexing the score
matrix to the labelled classes before any selection, so the two cannot drift apart. The
consequence is that the numbers reported for held-out classes are free of selection effects
from $\lambda$, $n_{\mathrm{cal}}$ and $c$. The numbers reported for labelled classes are not,
which is the second reason the two tables are kept apart.

\subsection{Coverage guarantee}
\label{sec:guarantee}

Let $\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{fit}}$ and $\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{rec}}$ be disjoint sets of
calibration rows, of sizes $n_{\mathrm{fit}}$ and $n_{\mathrm{rec}}$.

\begin{proposition}[Marginal coverage is preserved]
\label{prop:marginal}
Let $(X_i,Y_i)_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{rec}}}$ and $(X_{n+1},Y_{n+1})$ be exchangeable, and
let $\tilde\delta_\cdot$ be any function of $\varphi(\cdot)$ and of
$(X_i,Y_i)_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{fit}}}$. Let $c$ be the conformal quantile at level
$\lceil (n_{\mathrm{rec}}+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil/n_{\mathrm{rec}}$ of the shifted scores
$\{s(X_i,Y_i)-\tilde\delta_{Y_i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{rec}}}$. Then
$\mathbb{P}\bigl(Y_{n+1}\in\mathcal{C}(X_{n+1})\bigr)\ge 1-\alpha$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Define $\tilde s(x,y)=s(x,y)-\tilde\delta_y$. Since $\tilde\delta_\cdot$ is a function of
$\varphi(\cdot)$ alone, and $\varphi$ is computed from the model's weights and does not involve
these calibration rows, $\tilde s$ is a \emph{fixed} score function with respect to the
sample. Exchangeability is closed under applying a fixed map pointwise, so
$\tilde s(X_1,Y_1),\dots,\tilde s(X_{n+1},Y_{n+1})$ are exchangeable and the standard split
conformal argument applies verbatim to $\tilde s$. Membership in $\mathcal{C}$ is exactly the
event $\tilde s(X_{n+1},Y_{n+1})\le c$ up to the constant shift. Full proof in
\cref{app:proof}.
\end{proof}

The proposition holds because only \emph{one} degree of freedom is calibrated on
$\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{rec}}$. $\tilde\delta_y$ may be arbitrarily complex, since it merely
redefines the score.

\paragraph{Reuse of calibration rows.} The premise names two disjoint row sets, and the
configuration used for every table in \cref{sec:exp} does not provide them: $g_\theta$,
$\lambda$ and $c$ are all estimated on the same calibration rows, so $\tilde\delta$ and $c$ are
dependent and the proposition does not apply as stated. We report the size of the resulting
optimism instead of assuming it away. On ten ImageNet splits, at the thresholds as they are
emitted, the marginal threshold covers $0.9013$ and \textsc{Pcc} covers $0.8989$ against a
target of $0.900$, so the reuse costs about $0.0024$ of marginal coverage, roughly thirty times
the finite-sample slack $1/(n+1)$ at our calibration sizes. \textsc{Pcc}-split, which divides
the calibration rows in two and satisfies the premise exactly, is reported in
\cref{app:split}.

\paragraph{Scope of the claim.} There is no finite-sample class-conditional guarantee for a
class with $n_y=0$, and there cannot be one for any method, since with no labelled example of
the class there is nothing to condition on. Our claim for those classes is empirical, namely
measured worst-class coverage at matched set size, and \cref{sec:exp} reports it as such.
Furthermore $\lambda$ and $n_{\mathrm{cal}}$ are data-dependent choices made on the labelled
label space, which makes the labelled-class table optimistic; this is why the two tables are
kept apart.
