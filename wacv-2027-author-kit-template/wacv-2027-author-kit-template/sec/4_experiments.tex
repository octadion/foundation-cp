\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\paragraph{Protocol.} Unless stated otherwise we use ImageNet-1k
\cite{russakovsky2015imagenet} scores from a SimCLRv2 encoder with a linear probe
\cite{chen2020big}, descriptors from a supervised ResNet-50 head \cite{he2016deep} so that
$\varphi$ is exogenous to the scoring model, $\alpha=0.10$, $30\%$ of classes held out with
all of their calibration rows deleted, $n_{\mathrm{cal}}=25$, and five random splits.
Intervals are $95\%$ confidence intervals over splits, and every comparison is at matched
average set size (\cref{sec:setup}).

\paragraph{Metrics.} \Cref{tab:metrics} in the appendix defines every quantity. The headline
statistic is worst-class coverage, since one badly covered class is what a class-conditional
guarantee is meant to prevent. It can only be estimated when classes have enough evaluation
examples, so we fix a threshold of $30$ per class in advance: above it we report per-class
worst coverage (regime~A), below it a prevalence-binned surrogate (regime~B). Every result
states its regime, and \cref{sec:depth} shows why that matters.

\paragraph{Oracle ceiling.} To give the numbers a scale we report an oracle that observes the
test labels, forms the exact per-class offset, then selects its own shrinkage. It is
unattainable and is not a method; it says how much of the available room a method took.
Percentages of it are ratios of averages, because the ceiling itself moves by $0.10$ between
splits, so the fraction taken ranges from $2\%$ to $75\%$ split by split (\cref{app:oracle}).

\subsection{Classes with no calibration examples}
\label{sec:comp}

PCC improves worst-class coverage on the held-out classes by $0.075$, which is $70\%$ of the
oracle ceiling, while the best competitor reaches $0.027$ and six of the eight change the
statistic by exactly zero (\cref{tab:competitors}). Paired over splits, PCC exceeds those six
with intervals excluding zero; against the two moving \textsc{Fuzzy} variants the differences
are $0.049$ and $0.072$ but their intervals include zero at three splits.

Two of the zeros are not measurements: \textsc{Classwise} CP and \textsc{Rc3p} return an
infinite threshold for all $300$ classes, so their entry records the pre-registered fallback.
The other four are measurements, and \textsc{Interp-Q} is the instructive one. Its rule for an
undefined classwise quantile is to replace it with one, the largest value the softmax score can
take, and then blend, so a held-out class receives the widest threshold in the label space and
every other class pays for it once set size is matched back down. Sweeping the interpolation
weight and keeping the best, the selected weight is zero on every split, at which point the
method is \textsc{Standard} CP.

Prevalence is a weak signal here. \textsc{Fuzzy} CP with the rarity projection reaches $0.003$
and is beaten by the same method with a \emph{random} projection at $0.027$, so widening
thresholds for data-poor classes in an arbitrary direction already buys more than prevalence
does. Our feature ablation agrees independently, since prevalence-only descriptors drive
$\lambda$ to zero in all ten splits (\cref{tab:ablation}). The descriptors of \cref{sec:phi}
buy $2.8$ times what a random direction buys.

The gain is paid for. The correction lifts the worst class from $0.569$ to $0.647$ and leaves
macro-coverage at $0.898$ against $0.896$, but because set size is fixed, $12\%$ of classes
gain coverage while $27\%$ lose a little (\cref{app:whohelped}).


\subsection{Ablations}
\label{sec:ablations}

Shrinkage carries the method. Both endpoints of the $\lambda$ grid are significantly worse
than the selected interior value, and the unshrunk correction costs $0.584$ in worst-class
coverage (\cref{tab:ablation}). Among descriptors, distances alone and prevalence alone are
both significantly worse than the full set, while removing prevalence is not ($p=0.30$), so
the geometry carries the signal.

The last two rows are exactly zero for a reason that belongs to the metric itself. Adding a constant to every threshold is cancelled exactly by the size-matching
shift, and marginal recalibration is such a constant. At the thresholds as emitted it moves
marginal coverage from $0.8963$ to $0.8989$ against a target of $0.900$, at a cost of $0.31$
in set size. \Cref{app:invisible} gives that view in full.

\subsection{The role of evaluation depth}
\label{sec:depth}

Applied without modification, PCC fails on two long-tailed datasets and two additional
backbones. All four failures lie on one axis, and finding it meant discarding two explanations
we had held. Calibration depth is not the cause. Capping calibration examples inside ImageNet
gives a monotone dose-response from $0.033$ at ten per class to $0.060$ at one hundred, with
every interval excluding zero (\cref{tab:depth}), and ten per class is far thinner than the
settings that fail. Descriptors endogenous to the scoring model are not the cause either:
replacing each backbone's own head with a fixed exogenous one leaves every interval containing
zero (\cref{app:backbones}).

\Cref{fig:sweeps}a caps the number of \emph{evaluation} examples per class instead. The
effect is monotone and its interval crosses zero between $35$ and $75$. More importantly the
oracle ceiling collapses on the same axis, reaching zero by three examples per class, where
even a method holding the test labels could not improve worst-class coverage. What disappears
there is the room, and not the ability to take it. The axis predicts the four failures
quantitatively. The additional backbones sit at $35$ and measure $0.017$, which is what the
sweep gives at $35$; Pl@ntNet-300K sits at three and iNaturalist-2018 at two, where the sweep
is at or below zero.

That explanation is falsifiable, and restricting evaluation to classes above the threshold
confirms it on Pl@ntNet-300K. Of its $1081$ classes, $98$ have at least $75$ evaluation
examples and $34$ of those are held out; over that label space the improvement is $0.019$
$[0.010,0.028]$, five splits of five. At the weaker threshold of $35$ it does not reach
significance, $0.020$ $[-0.011,0.052]$, which is what \cref{fig:sweeps}a predicts at the
edge of measurability. The apparent failure was an artefact of what the test set can measure,
and the price of removing it is that the claim covers under a tenth of the label space
(\cref{tab:longtail}).

iNaturalist-2018 stays at zero even after restriction, and there the cause is different.
$\lambda$ is selected as $0$ in every split, so the method returns the marginal threshold
untouched. The shrinkage curve is not flat, which would mean $\hat\delta$ is degenerate; it
decreases monotonically, so $\hat\delta$ carries signal that points the wrong way. The
regression is correspondingly poor, with cross-validated error $0.127$ against $0.036$ on
ImageNet. Eight times as many classes in a descriptor space of the same dimension is the
obvious explanation and richer descriptors the obvious remedy, but we have tested neither
(\cref{app:longtail}). What shrinkage delivers here is that a $3.5\times$ worse regression
costs nothing, because the method detects that it cannot help and abstains.

\subsection{Amount of supervision required}
\label{sec:supervision}

Sweeping the held-out fraction from $10\%$ to $90\%$, the effect peaks at $50\%$ with $0.080$,
or $62\%$ of the ceiling, and remains significant at $90\%$, where only $100$ classes retain
labels, at $0.052$ $[0.007,0.098]$ (\cref{fig:sweeps}b). Four of the five points exclude zero;
the exception is $10\%$, where only $100$ classes are held out so the worst-class statistic
over them is noisier and the room is smaller. The floor is structural: $g_\theta$ is a ridge
over $p$ features and needs at least $p+2$ labelled classes, eleven here.

\subsection{Score functions and miscoverage level}
\label{sec:scores}

The correction survives every score but is strongest with LAC and attenuates for the adaptive
ones (\cref{tab:depth}). A plausible mechanism is that APS, RAPS and SAPS already reshape the
score using rank or cumulative mass, absorbing part of the class-difficulty signal that
$\delta_y$ captures. At $\alpha=0.01$ the effect is exactly zero, consistent with the
per-class offsets becoming indistinguishable from noise in the extreme tail.

PAS \cite{ding2026conformal} is included because it is the other published construction that
stays defined for a class with no calibration examples, and because a reader may ask whether
it removes the need for a per-class correction. It does not, though the reading is not a clean
win either: on PAS scores our improvement is $0.066$ with an interval containing zero, and the
ceiling rises to $0.234$, so PAS opens more room than LAC and we take a smaller share of it,
$28\%$ against $42\%$. Both fit prevalence carrying real but coarse information about class
difficulty, which \cref{tab:ablation} finds from the descriptor side.

\paragraph{Corrupted inputs.}
\label{sec:shift}
Calibrating on clean images and evaluating on ImageNet-C \cite{hendrycks2019benchmarking},
over $15$ corruptions at two severities, breaks exchangeability, so the guarantee is void for
every method compared. At contrast severity $5$, marginal coverage is $0.053$ and $75.7\%$ of
test points receive an empty set. Pooled over the $90$ conditions PCC gives $0.003$
$[0.001,0.005]$, an order of magnitude below the clean setting. We make no coverage claim here
(\cref{app:imagenetc}).

\paragraph{Computational cost.} PCC trains nothing. It is a closed-form ridge fit over at most
$K$ points with $p=9$ features, on descriptors read from the classifier's weights, so no
images, gradients or GPU are involved (\cref{app:runtime}).
